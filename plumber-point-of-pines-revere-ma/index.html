The neighborhood

From a resort neck to a residential end of the beach

Point of Pines was an isolated neck of land when a tavern called the Robinson Crusoe House opened there in 1834 and became a well-known resort. The Pines Hotel followed in 1881, and streetcars later ran along Revere Beach Boulevard to the Point.

The Massachusetts Historical Commission notes that summer houses in a range of styles were built along the Boulevard and at Point of Pines between 1915 and 1940. By the mid-twentieth century it was an established residential area.

Wikipedia describes the area as mainly single- and multi-family homes. That shapes the plumbing questions. Most calls from a house like this are about the basement, the water heater, outdoor fittings and the pipe that brings water in from the street.

Local note

The Pines River edge, the 1932 bridge and tidal marsh nearby

The Pines River forms the northern boundary of Revere. An Art Deco draw bridge carries Route 1A over it, built in 1932. Oak Island, a neighboring area of mostly single- and multi-family homes, borders Revere's marsh and wetlands.

The City of Revere flood-readiness page offers High Water and King Tide text alerts and lists flood sensors at Rumney Marsh, Belle Isle Marsh and other sites. It recommends sump pumps and backflow preventers for basements. Sign up for the alerts if your street sits low.

Sources: Massachusetts Historical Commission Revere community report (1981, so dated). Wikipedia neighborhood summary for Revere. City of Revere flood-readiness page (municipal source).

Checklist

Pipes and fittings worth a look in a waterfront house

General guidance, not a finding about any one building. Exposure varies from one house to the next.

Backflow protection

A backflow preventer on the sewer line helps keep street water out of floor drains. Ask whether your line has one.

Sump pump and discharge

Test the pump and make sure the discharge ends outside and away from the foundation.

Outdoor spigots and hose bibs

Shut off and drain exterior lines before freezing weather. Look for drips on a quiet night.

The water service line

A sudden drop in pressure or a wet patch in the yard can point to the line from the street. Call before it worsens.

Before storms

A simple storm routine for a low-lying house

Check the pump the week before a forecast storm, not during it. Clear leaves from nearby street drains if it is safe, and keep valuables off the basement floor.

If water is rising, stay out of the basement and keep power off in wet areas. Report street flooding to Revere 311 at 781-286-8311, and call a plumber for anything inside the house.

FAQ

Plumbing questions in Point of Pines

Do you serve Point of Pines in Revere?

Yes. We take calls from Point of Pines from our base on Revere Beach Parkway. Call (617) 752-0573 and the dispatcher will confirm timing for your address.

Is Point of Pines the same as Oak Island?

No. They are neighboring areas near the end of Revere Beach. Both have mostly single- and multi-family homes beside the marsh and wetlands, and we take calls from either.

Do I need a backflow preventer?

The city recommends them for basements, but whether your house needs one depends on your sewer line and basement drains. A plumber can inspect the line and explain the options.

Can I test my sump pump myself?

Yes. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start, empty it and shut off. If it does not, call a plumber before the next storm.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in Revere?

Many jobs do. According to the state plumbers and gas fitters board, permits go only to licensed plumbers. Ask the plumber whether your job needs one before work starts.
