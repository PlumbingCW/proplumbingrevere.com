Diagnosis

No hot water: what usually fails first

On a gas tank, the common causes are a pilot that has gone out, a failed thermocouple or a dirty burner. On an electric tank, a tripped breaker, a failed upper or lower heating element or a bad thermostat is more likely. Tankless units add error codes and flow sensors to the list.

The plumber checks power or gas first, then the controls, then the parts that wear. A short test run shows whether the heater now holds temperature under load. Many repairs finish on the first visit, and the plumber explains the price of each fix before work starts.

Leak response

Leaking water heater: shut it down first

Water at the base of a tank can come from a loose fitting, a relief valve or a rusted tank. Only a plumber can tell which, and in the meantime the goal is to stop the flow and avoid injury.

Cold water valve

Close the supply valve on the pipe entering the top of the heater. This stops the tank from refilling.

Power or gas

Switch the breaker off for an electric unit. For gas, set the control to off. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or National Grid.

Do not touch hot pipes

The relief valve and outlet pipe can discharge scalding water. Keep children and pets away.

Revere water

What Revere's soft water means for your heater

Revere is supplied by the MWRA, which describes its water as soft, at about 16 mg/L of hardness. Scale builds slowly in tanks and heating elements, so sediment is less often the reason a Revere heater fails early.

The MWRA treats its water to a pH of about 9.0 to 9.5 to reduce metal leaching. When a tank fails here, the cause is usually age, a worn anode rod or a failed part, not a hardness problem. You do not need a softener to protect the heater, and a salesperson who says otherwise should show you a water test.

Rules

Permits and licensed work on a water heater

According to the state plumbers and gas fitters board, only a licensed plumber may repair or replace plumbing, and the local plumbing inspector issues a permit before the work. In Revere the Building Division issues plumbing and gas permits only to licensed plumbers and gas fitters, so a homeowner cannot apply.

The electrical side of an electric heater falls under the Massachusetts Electrical Code, 527 CMR 12.00, which is based on the National Electrical Code (NEC). A new circuit or a damaged one may need a licensed electrician, and the plumber will tell you when that applies.

A simple repair such as a thermostat may not need a new permit. The rule varies by job, so ask the Revere Building Division at 781-286-8196 or let your plumber confirm. If you rent, tell the landlord because the heater is usually theirs to maintain.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Revere?

Yes. The line is answered around the clock. A dispatcher can send a licensed plumber the same day for a leaking tank or no hot water, and will tell you how to shut the heater down while you wait.

Is a pilot light that keeps going out dangerous?

It can be. A failing thermocouple, a draft or a venting problem can all be the cause. Do not keep relighting it. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid.

How long does a water heater repair take?

Most diagnoses take under an hour. Common repairs often finish on the same visit if the plumber has the part. Special parts can need a second trip.

Can you repair a tankless water heater?

Yes. Error codes, flow sensors, igniters and clogged inlet screens are common. See our tankless water heater page for flushing and service.

Does the repair need a permit in Revere?

Permit rules vary by job. Revere issues plumbing and gas permits to licensed plumbers only, through its e-permitting portal. Your plumber will confirm what applies.

Should I repair or replace an old tank?

If the tank is leaking from the shell, replace it. If the tank is old and the repair is large, compare the repair price with replacement.
