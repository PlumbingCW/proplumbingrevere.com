Water Heater Anode Rod Replacement in Revere, MA

An anode rod is a metal rod inside a tank water heater that corrodes first so the tank steel does not. Check it every few years. Replace it when it is thin, coated or mostly gone, because after that the tank itself starts to rust.

In Revere, the water is soft and the treated pH is high, so rods can wear differently than in hard-water towns. The only way to know is to pull it and look.

    What it does

    Why a heater needs a sacrificial rod

    Water attacks steel. The anode rod is made of magnesium, aluminum or a zinc blend, and it is more reactive than the tank, so it corrodes first. As long as some rod remains, the tank is protected.

    When the rod is used up, corrosion shifts to the tank walls. A rusty tank leaks and cannot be repaired, so the whole heater is replaced.

    Rod types

    Magnesium, aluminum and powered rods

    TypeGood forWatch for

    Check and swap

    How a rod gets inspected and replaced

    This is a heavy, awkward job, and many rods are stuck tight. A plumber usually has the tools to free them.

    1

    Shut off power or gas

    Turn off the heater and the cold supply, then drain a few gallons.

    2

    Loosen the rod

    Use a long socket or breaker bar on the hex head at the top of the tank.

    3

    Inspect

    A rod thinner than a pencil in places, or with steel showing, needs replacing.

    4

    Install and refill

    Wrap the threads with tape, fit the new rod, refill and check for leaks.

    Local note

    Local note: soft MWRA water and the rod

    According to the MWRA, Revere's water is soft at about 16 mg/L of hardness and about 1 grain per gallon. The treated water has a pH of about 9.0 to 9.5, adjusted to reduce metal leaching from household plumbing.

    The MWRA does not say what its water does to a tank rod. Rod wear depends on the rod metal, the temperature setting and the tank, so a visual check is the reliable test. Many plumbers suggest looking at the rod every two to three years.

    Soft water also means mineral scale is slow, so sediment in a Revere tank is often fine debris rather than hard scale.

    Sources: MWRA hardness and drinking water quality pages (utility source).

    Decision

    When a new rod is not worth it

    A rod replacement makes sense on a younger tank that has been neglected. It does not make sense on an old tank that already leaks, shows rust at the seams or has rusty water at the tap.

    If you see water pooling under the tank, do not wait. See emergency water heater replacement, and neighbors in Chelsea can use our Chelsea page.

    Warning signs

    Signs a rod may be used up

    Rusty or brown hot water, a metallic taste, a popping or rumbling tank and a rotten egg smell can all be linked to a worn rod, though other problems cause them too. A plumber can tell the difference by checking the rod and the tank.

    If only the hot side is rusty, the tank is the likely source. If the cold side is also rusty, the problem may be in the supply pipes instead.

    Space and tools

    Why this job is harder than it looks

    The rod sits under a hex head on top of the tank. Many basements leave only a few inches of clearance, so a straight rod cannot be pulled out. A flexible rod bends to fit, and a plumber can fit it with less force.

    Old rods can be seized by years of corrosion. Using too much force can damage the tank or the fittings, so stop if the head will not turn and call a plumber.

    Cost

    Rod replacement or a new heater

    A rod and labor usually cost much less than a new heater. A rod can extend the life of a younger tank. It cannot help an old tank that has already corroded through.

    Ask the plumber to look at the tank age on the data plate, the condition of the fittings and any rust at the base. Put all of that next to the rod condition and decide together.

    Records

    Keep a simple heater log

    Write down the install date, the rod inspection dates and any repairs. A short log helps a plumber judge the remaining life of the tank. Keep the data plate details, such as the model and serial number, in a phone photo.

    Set the temperature no higher than needed for your household. Hotter settings speed up corrosion and scale, and they raise scald risk for children and older adults.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    How often should I check the anode rod?

    Many plumbers suggest every two to three years, or sooner in a heater that has had problems.

    Can a worn rod cause smelly water?

    Some rods react with bacteria and give a rotten egg smell. A plumber can try a different rod type.

    Does soft water use up a rod faster?

    It can in some cases. The MWRA says its water is soft, so check the rod rather than guessing.

    Is replacing the rod a DIY job?

    It can be, but a seized rod, tight clearance or a gas heater make it a job for a plumber.

    Does a new rod void my warranty?

    Usually not, but read the warranty. Some require a licensed installer for service work.
