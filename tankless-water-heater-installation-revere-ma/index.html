Tankless Water Heater Installation in Revere, MA

A tankless install is more than a swap. Most gas models need a larger gas line than the old tank, a sealed vent, a condensate drain and often a new power outlet. A plumber checks each one before you buy the unit.

In Revere, where many homes are old and basements are tight, the venting path is often the deciding factor. Plan for a site check first.

    Site check

    Five things a plumber measures first

    Gas line size

    Tankless units burn more gas at once. The line may need to be larger.

    Vent path

    Condensing units use plastic vent pipe, and the route to the outside must be short enough.

    Condensate drain

    The unit makes acidic water that must drain, often through a neutralizer.

    Power

    A small outlet runs the fan and controls.

    Water lines

    Isolation valves go on so the unit can be flushed later.

    Local note

    Local note: gas work and permits in Revere

    According to the Revere Building Division, plumbing and gas permits go only to Massachusetts licensed plumbers, and homeowners cannot apply. Permits are filed through the division's online system, and inspections follow.

    The state plumbers and gas fitters board says gas fitting needs a licensed gas fitter or plumber, and the board verifies licenses at 617-727-9952. Ask any installer for a license number and check it.

    If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid at 1-800-233-5325. Do not switch lights or use a phone inside.

    Sources: City of Revere Building Division page (municipal source) and the Massachusetts plumbers and gas fitters board fact sheet (state source).

    Rebates and maintenance

    After the install

    Rebates change, so ask before you buy. Mass Save lists rebates for heat pump water heaters, and the program terms differ by equipment type. Gas tankless units need a periodic flush, and a plumber can add the valves during the install.

    If you would rather compare, see water heater installation, and our Chelsea page if you are across the creek.

    Sizing

    How a plumber sizes a tankless unit

    A plumber adds up the fixtures that may run together, such as a shower, a dishwasher and a washer. They also consider how cold the incoming water is, because the unit must raise it to the temperature you want.

    A unit that is too small gives lukewarm water when two fixtures run. A unit that is much bigger than needed costs more and may need a bigger gas line. Ask the plumber to explain how they sized it.

    Budget

    Cost factors to ask about

    The price depends on the unit, the gas line, the vent route, the condensate drain, the electrical work and the permit. A clear price should list each one. Ask whether the old vent will be removed and how the wall will be patched.

    Ask about the maker's warranty and who services the unit. A licensed plumber should explain what the labor warranty covers.

    Old homes

    Challenges in older Revere homes

    Old basements often have low ceilings, tight walls and chimneys that no longer serve a gas heater. A tankless unit needs a clear route for a vent, and the plumber may use a wall or a side vent.

    Pipes may be older too. A plumber checks the water lines and valves while the unit is open, and may recommend replacing worn parts at the same time.

    Permits

    What to ask the installer about the paperwork

    Ask who files the permit and who schedules the inspection. Ask for a copy of the permit and the final sign-off for your records. A licensed plumber should handle both without asking you to apply.

    Keep the manual, the warranty card and the installer's contact details in one folder. If a problem shows up later, you will have everything in one place.

    Comfort

    Hot water behavior after the switch

    A tankless unit gives hot water for as long as it runs, but there is a short wait while it fires up. A recirculation loop can shorten that wait if the house has a return line. Ask the plumber whether one fits your layout.

    Very low flows may not start the unit, so a trickle from a faucet can stay cold. This is normal for many models and is one reason sizing and fixtures matter.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    How long does a tankless install take?

    A like-for-like swap takes a day. A first conversion with new gas and vent can take longer.

    Do I need a bigger gas line?

    Often yes. A tankless unit draws more gas at once than a tank. A plumber sizes it.

    Can I vent through the old chimney?

    Sometimes, with a liner. It depends on the model and the chimney, so a plumber checks.

    Does Revere require a permit?

    The Building Division says plumbing and gas permits go only to licensed plumbers. Ask the division what your job needs.

    Will I lose hot water if the power goes out?

    Most tankless units need power for the fan and controls, so hot water stops in an outage.
