Scope

What counts as a commercial plumbing job

Commercial work includes restaurant kitchens and dishwashers, restrooms with multiple fixtures, water heaters sized for staff and customers, and floor drains in garages and back rooms. It also covers common plumbing in apartment buildings, where one stack can feed several households.

Revere is a good example of the mix. According to the city's housing plan, 22 percent of homes are in buildings of ten or more units and 17 percent in buildings of three to nine, so many addresses have shared plumbing that behaves like a commercial system.

Business emergencies

Plumbing emergency at your business: first steps

The first call is to the 24/7 line. The dispatcher sends a licensed plumber. Here is what to do while you wait to limit the damage.

Isolate the problem

Close the shutoff for the fixture. If it is a main or riser, close the building main.

Protect people

Cone off wet floors and close affected restrooms or kitchen areas.

Protect stock and equipment

Move paper goods, food and electronics off the floor.

Tell the landlord

In leased space, the lease may state who handles which pipes. Notify the property manager.

Corridors

Working in Revere's retail and mixed-use districts

The Squire Road district in West Revere has shopping plazas, restaurants and hotels, and Broadway and Revere Beach Parkway carry more storefronts. Tenants in these buildings usually cannot work inside business hours, so evening and overnight plumbing work is common.

Revere sets permitted construction hours from Monday to Saturday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m., with work outside these hours needing a special permit from the Building Commissioner. Ask your plumber how that applies to scheduled work, because an emergency repair and a planned renovation are handled differently.

Compliance

Grease, backflow and licensed work

Restaurants need grease traps or interceptors maintained, and a backed-up trap causes the kind of kitchen emergency that closes a dining room. Cleaning and servicing a trap is part of commercial plumbing. We treat grease trap cleaning as a section of this page, not a separate service.

Backflow prevention assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination. They need periodic testing, and a failed test means repair or replacement. Whatever the job, Massachusetts requires licensed plumbers, and the Revere Building Division issues permits only to them.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle commercial plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in Revere?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day. A licensed plumber can be dispatched after hours to restaurants, retail units, offices and apartment buildings.

Do you service restaurant grease traps?

Yes. Cleaning and servicing grease traps is part of commercial plumbing, and a plumber can set a regular schedule.

Can you work around my business hours?

Often yes. Planned work can be scheduled early or late. Revere's construction hours apply, so ask about special permits.

Who is responsible for plumbing in a rented space?

The lease decides. Many leases make the landlord responsible for the building system and the tenant for the fixtures. Check the lease and tell the property manager.

Do you test backflow assemblies?

Backflow testing is a part of commercial work. The assembly must be tested by a licensed professional, and failed tests need repair.

Do you serve apartment buildings?

Yes. Stacks, risers, boilers, water heaters and common drains in multi-unit buildings are part of commercial plumbing.
