The neighborhood

Beachmont homes: Victorian cottages, two-families and apartments

Beachmont was the first part of Revere to develop in a big way. The Boston Land Company bought the James Sale Farm there in 1872, and a narrow gauge railroad opened in 1875 to link the area with Boston and Lynn.

The Massachusetts Historical Commission describes well-detailed suburban cottages in the Stick, Second Empire and Queen Anne styles, some probably built as summer cottages. Later infill added two- and three-family houses and apartment buildings.

We do not guess pipe material from a building's age. A century-old house may have been repiped twice, and a newer addition may hide an old drain. A plumber should open the access point and look before recommending a repair.

Local note

Flood alerts, Pearl Avenue and the Beachmont Resilience Project

The City of Revere flood-readiness page lists Hohonu flood sensors at Belle Isle Marsh and Pearl Avenue, among other sites, with High Water and King Tide text alerts. Both places are on the Beachmont side of the city, so these alerts are worth a look if you live nearby.

Local papers reported in February 2025 that city officials met with Beachmont residents about the Beachmont Resilience Project. We have not seen final design details, so we do not describe what the project will build or when.

To report a clogged street catch basin or a drainage problem outside your property, use Revere 311 at 781-286-8311. A plumber handles the pipes on your side of the property line.

Sources: City of Revere flood-readiness page (municipal source). Revere Journal and Eastie Times coverage of the February 2025 resident meeting, headline level only.

Checklist

What to check in a Beachmont basement or utility room

General guidance, not a finding about any one building. Use it as a starting list before you call.

The sump pit

If you have a pit, pour in a bucket of water and watch the pump start and empty it. Check that the discharge pipe ends outside.

Floor drains

A floor drain that gurgles in a storm may be a sewer backup. Keep power off in wet areas and call a plumber.

The water heater

Look for rust at the base, a damp floor or a pan that holds water. Note the age on the label.

The main shutoff

Find it before you need it, and make sure everyone in a shared building knows where it is.

Renting or owning

Who to call first in a two- or three-family building

In a rental, tell the landlord or building manager first, because repairs to shared pipes are usually the owner's decision. Take a photo of any damage and note when it started.

In a condo, ask management before work that touches a common wall, riser or shutoff. Owners can still call a plumber for an urgent leak, but the association may need to know afterward.

FAQ

Plumbing questions in Beachmont

Do you serve Beachmont in Revere?

Yes. Our base is on Revere Beach Parkway in Revere, and we take calls from Beachmont. Call (617) 752-0573 and the dispatcher will confirm timing for your street.

Is my basement flooding a plumbing problem or a drainage problem?

Both are possible. A failed sump pump or backed-up floor drain is a plumbing job. Water in the street or a blocked catch basin is a city matter, which you can report to Revere 311.

Should I test my sump pump before a storm?

Yes. Pour water into the pit and confirm the pump starts, empties it and shuts off. Check that the discharge pipe is clear and ends outside the house.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in Revere?

Many jobs do. According to the state plumbers and gas fitters board, permits go only to licensed plumbers. Ask the plumber whether your job needs one before work starts.
