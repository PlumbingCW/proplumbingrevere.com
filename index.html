Why Revere plumbing fails the way it does

Revere sits on the north shore of Boston Harbor, with Revere Beach on the east and the Rumney Marsh Reservation on the north. According to the city's housing plan, most homes were built before 1960, and the largest group before 1940. That means old iron drains, galvanized supply lines and mixed repairs in the same basement.

The housing mix matters when a plumber diagnoses a problem. About 34 percent of homes are single-family, 27 percent are two-family, 17 percent sit in buildings of three to nine units and 22 percent in buildings of ten or more, based on 2012 to 2016 Census data in the city plan. A stoppage in a shared stack can show up two floors from where it started.

Roughly half of Revere households rent. If you rent and the problem is a toilet, a faucet or a water heater, tell your landlord right away. If water is actively pouring, shut off the nearest valve and call the emergency line first.

Soft water, lead pipes and what the city can tell you

Revere drinking water comes from the MWRA, which draws on the Quabbin and Wachusett Reservoirs. The MWRA describes its water as soft, at about 16 mg/L of hardness, so scale is rarely the problem and a softener is rarely needed. The treated water runs at a pH of about 9.0 to 9.5, which is adjusted to reduce lead and copper leaching from plumbing.

Lead still matters in older service lines. Revere reports 362 known lead service lines and 254 of unknown material in its inventory, and it removed 62 lead services in 2025 with 80 more planned for 2026. If your house was built before 1960, ask the city what is on record for your service line before you plan a repipe.

Our water service line repair page explains what a replacement involves, and the repiping page covers what to do inside the house.

When the harbor and the storm drains push back

Revere flooding is a local fact, not a rare event. The city flood-readiness page names Beachmont, bordering Belle Isle Marsh, and the Point of Pines and Riverside area as the parts most exposed. It recommends sump pumps and backflow preventers for basements. A sump pump that fails during a storm tide is a true emergency.

Sewer backups are a separate problem. The city settled a 2010 federal consent decree over sewage overflows and basement backups, and it has since built new drainage. Your own sewer lateral is still your responsibility from the street main to the house, and our sewer line repair and sewer inspection pages explain the options.

Shirley Avenue

FAQ

Emergency plumber questions in Revere

Is there an emergency plumber in Revere open at night and on weekends?

Yes. The emergency line is answered 24 hours a day, every day, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call (617) 752-0573, tell the dispatcher your address and what is happening, and a licensed plumber is sent as soon as one is available.

What should I do first if a pipe bursts in my Revere home?

Shut off the main water valve, turn off power to any wet area if you can reach the panel safely, and keep people away from the water. Then call the 24/7 line. In many Revere triple-deckers the main valve sits in the basement near the meter, so find it before you need it.

Do you serve the whole city, from Beachmont to Point of Pines?

Yes. We dispatch across Revere, including Beachmont, Shirley Avenue, West Revere and Squire Road, Oak Island and Point of Pines. We also cover Chelsea, Everett, Winthrop, Malden, Saugus and East Boston.

Can a homeowner pull a plumbing permit in Revere?

No. The Revere Building Division states that plumbing and gas permits can only be issued to Massachusetts licensed plumbers and gas fitters, and homeowners cannot apply for them. A licensed plumber applies through the city e-permitting portal before the work starts.

Do I need a water softener in Revere?

Usually not. Revere is supplied by the MWRA, which describes its water as soft, at about 16 mg/L of hardness or roughly 1 grain per gallon. If a salesperson says your tap water needs softening, ask for a test result first.

How do I check that a plumber is licensed in Massachusetts?

Call the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters at 617-727-9952 or use its online license lookup. The board confirms licensure, years licensed and any discipline. Any plumber should be able to show you a license on request.
