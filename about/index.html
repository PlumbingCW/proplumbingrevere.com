What we do

You call one number at any hour. A dispatcher records the address and the problem, tells you how to make the situation safe and sends a licensed plumber.

Work covers the services listed on our services page. That means drains, water heaters, leaks, sewer lines, sump pumps, toilets, faucets and more, for homes and small businesses.

The model is simple on purpose. One phone number, one conversation and a plumber at your door. There is no online form to fill in and no inbox to wait in.

How a call works

The dispatcher asks four things. Where are you, what is happening, is water or sewage still flowing and do you know where the main shutoff valve is. The answers decide whether the call is an emergency or can be booked for later.

For an emergency, you get shut-off instructions while the plumber is on the way. For gas, the first instruction is always to leave the building and call 911 or National Grid. For a planned job such as a water heater installation, the dispatcher books a time that suits you. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

Once the plumber arrives, the order is the same every time. Diagnose first, explain what the problem is, give a clear price and start work only after you agree.

How we price

We do not quote a number over the phone for a problem nobody has seen. The plumber diagnoses first and gives you a clear price before work starts. If you do not want the work at that price, you can say no.

Costs depend on access, materials, the age of the pipes and whether a permit is needed. Every service page lists the main cost factors in plain words, so you know what to expect before you call.

We do not promise a fixed arrival time. Traffic on Route 1A, Route 16 and Broadway changes from hour to hour, and a promise we cannot keep is worse than none.

Licensing and permits

According to the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters, plumbing work requires a licensed master or journeyman plumber, and a permit before most repairs and replacements. We send licensed plumbers.

In Revere, the Building Division issues plumbing and gas permits only to licensed plumbers and gas fitters. Homeowners cannot apply. That is one more reason to ask the plumber for the permit number when a job needs one.

You can check any plumber yourself. Call the board at 617-727-9952 or use its online lookup. It confirms licensure, years licensed and discipline. Do this for any contractor who works in your home, including us.

Why local knowledge matters

Revere plumbing is not generic. According to the city's housing plan, most homes were built before 1960 and the largest group before 1940. The same plan counts about a third of homes as single-family, 27 percent as two-family and 22 percent in buildings of ten or more units. Each type fails differently.

The water is local too. The MWRA supplies Revere with soft water at roughly 16 mg/L of hardness, and the city has lead service lines still on record. A plumber who knows this advises on repiping, water heaters and softeners without the sales script.

Flooding is the third local fact. The city flood-readiness page names Beachmont and the Point of Pines and Riverside area as the parts most exposed, and recommends sump pumps and backflow preventers. Our sump pump page explains what that means for a basement.

What we do not do

We do not handle gas leaks as the first call. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or National Grid first. We do not use stock reviews, invented ratings or made-up customer numbers on this site, because they would be false.

We also do not pressure anyone into work. A plumber who recommends replacing a pipe or a water heater should show you the reason, whether that is a camera image, a leak or the age of the equipment.

How we keep this site accurate

Local facts on this site come from city, state and utility pages, such as the Revere Building Division, the Revere Water and Sewer Department, the MWRA and Mass Save. When a figure cannot be confirmed, we leave it out or tell you to ask your utility.

Rules and rebate terms change. If you spot something out of date, email contact@proplumbingrevere.com and we will check it against the source.
