Overflow

Toilet overflowing: what to do first

An overflowing toilet puts water on the floor within seconds, and in a two-family or triple-decker it can reach the unit below. Act on the shutoff first, then call.

The line is answered 24 hours a day, including weekends. These steps stop the flow while you wait.

Close the flapper

Take the tank lid off and press the rubber flapper into its seat. This stops water entering the bowl.

Close the shutoff

Turn the oval valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

Do not flush again

More flushing adds water to a blocked line. If other fixtures gurgle too, the clog is in the main.

Tell the unit below

In a multi-family building, warn neighbors so they can watch their ceiling.

Installation

How a new toilet is installed

Installing a toilet is a short job when the floor and flange are sound. The plumber checks both first.

1

Shut off and remove

The water is closed, the tank and bowl are drained and the old toilet is lifted off.

2

Check the flange

The plumber inspects the floor flange for cracks or rot and repairs it if needed.

3

Set the new toilet

A new wax or rubber ring seals the bowl, and the bolts are tightened evenly.

4

Connect and test

The supply line is connected, the tank is filled and the toilet is flushed several times to test for leaks.

Rentals

Toilet repair for Revere renters and landlords

According to Revere's housing plan, about 51 percent of households rent and 48 percent own. That means many toilet calls come from tenants. Tell your landlord in writing when a toilet fails, and keep a copy.

The State Sanitary Code, Chapter II (105 CMR 410.000), sets minimum standards for rental housing, including working plumbing. Rules on who pays for what depend on the lease and the cause of damage, so ask your landlord or the local board of health. A plumber's written diagnosis helps either side.

Old houses

What older Revere bathrooms hide

Many Revere bathrooms were last remodeled long after the house was built, so a new toilet may sit on an old cast iron drain with a worn floor flange. A rocking toilet or a stain on the ceiling below can mean the flange has failed.

Small bathrooms also create clearance problems. A toilet with a larger tank or a different rough-in may not fit. The plumber measures the distance from the wall to the drain before you buy.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Revere?

Yes. The line is answered around the clock. A licensed plumber can come for an overflow or a leak, and the dispatcher tells you how to stop the water while you wait.

Is it easy to install a toilet myself?

The mechanical steps are simple, but a bad seal, a damaged flange or a heavy toilet can cause leaks and injury. A plumber also checks the drain. For a rental, ask the landlord first.

Why does my toilet run on and off by itself?

A worn flapper or a failing fill valve lets water leak from the tank to the bowl. The fix is usually quick, and fixing it stops wasted water.

Why is there water around the base of my toilet?

It can come from a failed wax ring, loose bolts or sweating on the tank. A plumber can tell which and reset the toilet if needed.

Do you remove the old toilet?

Yes. Removal and disposal are part of an installation job.

Can you fix a toilet that clogs every week?

Yes. A repeating clog often has a cause behind the bowl, such as a partial blockage or a damaged trap. Ask about a camera inspection.
