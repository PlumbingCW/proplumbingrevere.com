Active leak

When a hidden leak is an emergency

A leak that soaks drywall, drips through a light fixture or runs down a wall is an emergency. So is any wet spot near an electrical panel. Here is what to do first. Water travels along framing, so the stain you see may be several feet from the source.

These steps slow the damage while the plumber is on the way.

Close the main valve

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. In most Revere houses it is in the basement near the meter.

Keep power away from water

If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call. Do not stand in standing water to flip a breaker.

Catch and photograph

Place a bucket and photograph the stain, because adjusters ask for evidence.

Do not cut walls yet

Let the plumber locate the leak first. A random hole can miss it and cost more to repair.

Methods

How a plumber finds a leak without tearing out walls

The plumber starts with what you saw and heard, then tests the system in sections. Pressure gauges show whether the supply lines hold. Acoustic listening devices pick up the hiss of water escaping from a pipe, and thermal cameras show cold or warm lines behind a wall surface.

For a slab or an underground line, tracer gas or electronic line tracing narrows the location to a few feet. Most leaks are then opened in one small spot instead of a whole wall, and the plumber explains the repair before cutting.

High bills

High water bill in Revere: how to check for a leak

Revere has been replacing meters citywide with electronic Sensus iPERL meters that send readings to a radio on the outside of the building. According to the City of Revere, usage and billing data are available through a secure web application, and estimated readings will be replaced with actual ones.

If that record shows water running while nobody is home, you probably have a leak. A toilet flapper, a dripping hot water tank valve or a hidden pipe can each be the cause. The city also reports a leak detection system on its street mains. If your bill looks wrong, call the Revere Water Department at 781-286-8145 for the meter and a plumber for your piping.

Local conditions

Why Revere buildings leak in specific ways

Older Revere homes combine galvanized steel, copper and PEX in the same house. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside, and a pinhole leak often appears at a threaded joint. Copper can fail at solder joints or where it passes through masonry.

Multi-unit buildings add another problem. A leak in a stack or a riser can wet a unit below the one with the broken pipe. About 22 percent of Revere homes are in buildings of ten or more units, and 17 percent in buildings of three to nine, so shared lines are common. Tell the plumber which units are affected.

Winter is the other local factor. Freezing weather can split a pipe in an exposed wall, and the damage shows when the thaw starts.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you handle leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Revere?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day. A licensed plumber can come out when a leak is actively damaging a home, and the dispatcher tells you how to shut off the water while you wait.

Can you find a slab leak without breaking the floor?

Often yes. Listening equipment and pressure tests narrow the location. The floor is opened only at the spot the equipment points to.

How do I know a high water bill is a leak?

Check the water usage on the city's web application for flow when nobody is home. Also look for a toilet that keeps running or a faucet that drips.

Does the city fix a leak between the street and my house?

The property owner is responsible for the service line to the home, and the city handles the main. Ask the Water Department at 781-286-8145 where the boundary falls for your property.

Will insurance pay for leak detection?

It depends on the policy. Many cover damage from a sudden leak but not the cost of finding gradual leaks. Ask before work starts.

How long does leak detection take?

Many fixture and wall leaks are found in a visit. Slab or underground leaks can take longer.
