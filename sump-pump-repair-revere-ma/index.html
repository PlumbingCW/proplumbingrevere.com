Sump Pump Repair in Revere, MA: A Homeowner Guide

A sump pump that will not run usually has one of five problems. The power is off, the float switch is stuck, the intake or discharge is clogged, the check valve has failed or the motor has worn out. You can test the first three in a few minutes with the pump unplugged.

If the pit is full and the pump still will not start after those checks, call a plumber. In Revere, where a storm tide can fill a basement quickly, do not wait for the next rain.

    Checklist

    Five checks before you call a plumber

    Work through these in order. Unplug the pump before you touch anything in the pit, and do not reach in while the floor is wet around an outlet.

    1. Power: check that the pump is plugged into a working outlet and that the breaker has not tripped. A pump on a GFCI outlet can trip it when the pit is wet.
    2. Float: lift the float switch by hand. If the pump starts, the float was jammed against the pit wall or a pipe.
    3. Intake: if the pump runs but moves little water, silt or gravel may block the intake screen under the pump.
    4. Discharge: follow the pipe outside. Ice, a kinked hose or a clogged end can stop the flow.
    5. Check valve: if the pump cycles every few minutes, the valve above it may be letting water fall back into the pit.

    Repair or replace

    When repair stops making sense

    A pump that has lasted many years and now needs a new float, a new check valve and a clean-out is close to the end. A new pump costs more than those parts but removes the next failure. If you also have no backup power, replacing the pump is the moment to add one.

    A plumber can size the new pump to the pit, set it on a firm base and test the float with water. That visit usually takes a couple of hours.

    Routine

    A five-minute pump test before storm season

    1

    Pour in water

    Add several buckets to the pit until the float rises.

    2

    Watch the cycle

    The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off cleanly.

    3

    Check the outlet

    Walk outside and confirm water flows away from the house.

    4

    Test the backup

    If you have a battery backup, unplug the main pump and test it too.

    Where the water goes

    Keep the discharge out of the sewer

    Revere's Water and Sewer Department says connecting downspouts, foundation drains or other surface or groundwater sources to the sewer service is prohibited. If your pump drains into a floor drain, fix it when you repair the pump.

    The discharge pipe should end outside, far enough from the foundation that water does not run back. A basement waterproofing plan can add interior drains that feed the same pit. Chelsea homeowners with the same problem can use our Chelsea emergency plumber page.

    Backup

    Backup power and alarms for a storm night

    Most pump failures happen when the weather is worst. Storms cut power, and a pump with no backup stops when the grid does. A battery backup pump sits beside the main pump and takes over when power fails or the main pump cannot keep up.

    A water alarm is cheap and loud. Set it a few inches above the pump so it sounds before the pit overflows. Test the alarm and the backup battery when you test the pump, and replace the battery when the maker says to.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    Can a homeowner replace a sump pump?

    Often yes for a like-for-like swap, if you can safely reach the pit and shut off power. A plumber is the better choice when the pit, check valve or discharge also needs work.

    How do I know my sump pump is failing?

    Loud grinding, constant running, short cycling or a pit that fills while the pump sits idle all point to failure. Test it before wet weather.

    Does a battery backup help in a storm?

    Yes. Storms often cut power, and a backup runs the pump for a limited time. Test the battery regularly.

    Does Revere offer flood alerts for homeowners?

    Yes. The city lists High Water and King Tide text alerts connected to its Hohonu sensors. Check the city flood-readiness page to sign up.

    Is a sump pump the same as a backflow preventer?

    No. A sump pump removes groundwater from a pit. A backflow preventer stops sewage or street water coming back through your drains.
