Neighborhoods

Chelsea neighborhoods we dispatch to, from Bellingham Square to the waterfront

Chelsea is the smallest city in Massachusetts by land area, at about 2.2 square miles, and it is packed with homes and businesses. The city's neighborhoods include Bellingham Square, the Box District, Prattville, Mill Hill, Admirals Hill, Addison-Orange and the Waterfront District. Each is within reach of our emergency line at any hour.

Bellingham Square is the civic center, with City Hall and the public library. Admirals Hill holds the old Chelsea Naval Hospital, and the state-run Soldiers' Home stands on Powderhorn Hill. For a plumber, the lesson is the same across all of them. Buildings are close together, shared walls are common and a leak in one unit often reaches the next.

Housing

Housing built after two fires, and what it means for pipes

The Great Chelsea Fire of April 12, 1908 destroyed nearly half the city and left about 18,000 people homeless. Rebuilding took about two and a half years, and the city was redesigned with wider streets and better emergency access. A second large fire in 1973 burned 18 city blocks.

Many Chelsea buildings therefore date from the rebuilding periods or from later decades. We do not assume pipe material from a date. A plumber checks whether a building has cast iron drains, copper, galvanized steel or newer plastic and plans the repair from that. The 2020 Census counted 14,554 housing units in Chelsea, so the mix is wide.

Low ground

Low ground, storm backups and the city's backflow program

Chelsea is mostly low, with water on three sides from the Mystic River, Chelsea Creek and Mill Creek. Its elevation is about 9.8 feet. Heavy storms can overload street drains and push sewage back into basements.

The Chelsea Department of Public Works runs a Basement Drain Backflow Preventer Program. It may reimburse up to $2,500 for owner-occupied one- to three-family homes and condos, after the city confirms repeated sewer backups during storms. The owner must pay for televising the sewer service line first, hire a plumber and pass an Inspectional Services inspection before payment.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak: before we arrive

These steps work in any Chelsea building. Follow them in order while the plumber is on the way.

Shut the main valve

Find the main shutoff near the meter, often in the basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Kill power to wet areas

If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out. Shut off power only from dry ground.

Clear the area

Move people, pets and valuables away from the water and the ceiling below a leak.

Call the 24/7 line

Give the address, floor and what you see. The dispatcher sends a licensed plumber.

Water and sewer

Water, sewer and who to call besides a plumber

Chelsea's water comes through the MWRA, which describes its water as soft, at about 16 mg/L of hardness. You rarely need a softener here. The city's Water and Sewer Division handles the public system. Your plumber handles the pipes on your property.

The city publishes information on its combined sewer overflow program, which is why heavy storms test the street drains. If neighbors on your block back up at the same time, report it to the city as well as calling a plumber.

Rules

Licensing and permits when you hire a plumber in Chelsea

According to the state plumbers and gas fitters board, plumbing work needs a licensed master or journeyman plumber and a permit from the local inspector. Chelsea's program requires work to be inspected by Inspectional Services before the city pays any reimbursement.

Check any plumber's license with the state board at 617-727-9952. The board confirms licensure, years licensed and discipline. Do it before you sign anything, in Chelsea or anywhere else.

Neighbors

Beyond Chelsea: Revere, Everett and the north shore

Chelsea shares its borders with Revere to the north, Everett to the west and Boston to the south and east. Our home page explains how we serve Revere from the same base, and the service areas page lists the other nearby ZIP codes.

Chelsea also sits beside the Tobin Bridge, which carries Route 1 across the Mystic River to Charlestown. Traffic on the bridge changes arrival times at rush hour, which is why we never promise one.

FAQ

Common questions

Is emergency service available in Chelsea?

Yes. The emergency line is answered 24 hours a day, including nights, weekends and holidays. A licensed plumber is dispatched to Chelsea as soon as one is available, and the dispatcher tells you how to stop the water first.

Do you handle plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in Chelsea?

Yes. The emergency line is answered every hour of the day and night, including weekends and holidays. A licensed plumber is dispatched to Chelsea as soon as one is available.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Chelsea?

Chelsea uses a single ZIP code, 02150, which runs from Bellingham Square and Mill Hill down to the Waterfront District along Chelsea Creek. We also dispatch to neighboring 02151 in Revere, 02149 in Everett and 02128 in East Boston.

Does the city of Chelsea help pay for a backflow preventer?

The Chelsea Department of Public Works may reimburse up to $2,500 for owner-occupied one- to three-family homes and condos after it confirms repeated storm backups. The owner pays for the sewer televising and hires a plumber, and Inspectional Services must inspect the work.

What should I do if a basement drain backs up in a storm?

Stay out of the water, keep power off in the wet area and call the 24/7 line. Tell the dispatcher whether neighbors are affected, because that can point to the street system.

Do you serve Chelsea from Revere?

Yes. Our base is on Revere Beach Parkway in Revere, next to Chelsea across Mill Creek. We never promise an arrival time because bridge and street traffic change it.

Is Chelsea water hard or soft?

Soft. Chelsea receives MWRA water, which the MWRA describes as about 16 mg/L of hardness. A softener is rarely needed.
